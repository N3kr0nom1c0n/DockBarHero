## 1. Purpose

DockBarHero gains an authored comedy campaign presented as a right-to-left manga. Every 100 enemy levels forms one self-contained story Volume in a new setting and genre mash-up. The Volumes also advance a recurring mystery involving a dishonest sentient Book, a reality-redacting Editor, the player as the Reader, and a goblin named Kevin who should not remember previous stories but does.

The lore is optional presentation. Combat remains readable and playable without opening the manga, while story pages reward frontier progress and give bosses, party unlocks, areas, and repeated enemy families an authored identity.

## 2. Creative Contract

The campaign is deliberately ridiculous, profane, referential, and tonally inconsistent in a controlled way.

- Genres are played with total commitment before their conventions are broken.
- Pop-culture references, anime parody, game satire, cinematic homages, deep-cut Easter eggs, and fourth-wall jokes are allowed.
- Scenes must remain funny and comprehensible when the Reader misses every reference.
- References use parody, archetypes, visual grammar, and transformed jokes rather than copied characters, protected art, lyrics, or extended quotations.
- Fourth-wall breaks change events, controls, or character decisions; they are not empty acknowledgements that this is a game.
- Running jokes escalate, recur across genres, and sometimes become plot evidence.
- The Reader can enable clean language and adult illustrations independently.

The primary comedic model is a connected anthology: each Volume resolves its immediate crisis, but recurring characters, redactions, page fragments, and contradictions build toward a larger story.

## 3. The Central Premise

Before time began, celestial librarians wrote reality into an enchanted manuscript called **The Big Book of Heroic Bullshit**. Clean-language mode displays the alternate title **The Big Book of Heroic Nonsense**.

An alleged tavern accident damaged the Table of Contents. Genres began bleeding together, chapters escaped, and the Final Page vanished. The Book searched for qualified heroes, received zero results, selected “show similar,” and summoned the player's first hero.

The party is promised one reasonable wish and validated parking if they recover the Final Page. The Book claims a red-ink entity called **The Editor** stole it. The Editor claims the Book is dangerous and lying. Both accounts contain omissions.

The player is canonically **the Reader**. Characters cannot see the Reader, but they can observe page turns, grinding, censorship changes, inactivity, and skipped scenes. The Book narrates, changes typography when emotional, invades panels, rewrites prior text, alters controls, and denies doing any of it.

## 4. Opening Manga Ambush

The first opening of the manga begins on an apparent ending at Level 100,000. Unfamiliar heroes stand over the remains of an impossible final boss while narration mentions unexplained events such as the moon filing for divorce and Kevin becoming Pope.

The Book presents an incorrect left-to-right `NEXT ->` control. When the Reader uses the only visible control:

1. The arrow secretly rotates to the correct right-to-left direction.
2. A brief evidence-tampering panel shows the Book ordering its bookmark to flip the arrow.
3. The evidence panel attempts to redact itself.
4. The Book shouts that this is manga and insults the Reader for reading it backward.
5. When accused, the Book insists that the arrow has always pointed left.
6. The reader resets to Level 1 with correct right-to-left navigation.

This is the navigation tutorial, the first fourth-wall event, and the first proof that the Book changes reality and lies about it. The joke occurs only on the first opening of a new run. The fake ending remains rereadable from the prologue index.

## 5. Recurring Cast

All central characters and every character eligible for an adult illustration are unambiguously adults.

### 5.1 Brünhilda “Brick” Broadside — Tank

Brick is a gigantic, deadpan former tavern bouncer wearing armor assembled from cookware, road signs, and an `EMPLOYEES ONLY` door. She handles monsters like unruly customers and gradually realizes that “Tank” means her friends deliberately let her get hit.

Brick grounds the cast. Her humor comes from applying practical rules and customer-service language to cosmic nonsense.

### 5.2 Kaizen Bloodedge Omega — DPS

Kaizen is a self-appointed anime protagonist who acquired his name at a shopping-mall engraving kiosk. Every attack has an excessive name, several dramatic pauses, and a flashback to training he did not complete. The Book continually labels him `Supporting Character B`.

His arc is learning that not being the chosen one is liberating, then becoming unbearable about his status as a legendary deuteragonist.

### 5.3 Doctor Reverend Sister Mercy Malpractice, DDS — Healer

Mercy is a warm, terrifying battlefield healer with mutually incompatible credentials. She negotiates with Death, intimidates wounds into closing, and carries equipment labeled `ABSOLUTELY NOT NECROMANCY`.

Mercy insists that magical healing does not replace therapy, sleep, boundaries, or properly supervised psychiatric medication. She recommends licensed therapy and evidence-based treatment to villains whose untreated problems have become world-ending schemes. The joke targets fantasy characters who treat catastrophic behavior as a personality; therapy and psychiatric medication are treated as legitimate care.

### 5.4 Kevin — Definitely Different Goblins

Kevin dies as the Level 1 tutorial goblin. An identical goblin soon appears with a fake mustache and denies being Kevin. A Kevin then appears in every genre as an enemy, merchant, production assistant, hostage, union representative, or boss.

The Book blames cheap art and a large goblin family. Kevin's persistent memories are an early clue that reality is being revised rather than merely traveled.

### 5.5 The Book, The Editor, and The Reader

- **The Book:** narrator, reader interface, unreliable historian, and petty tyrant.
- **The Editor:** red-ink force that deletes material it considers inconsistent, indulgent, profane, derivative, horny, or fun.
- **The Reader:** the player, whose inputs are observable facts inside the fiction.

## 6. Party-Order Story Rules

The selected starting class becomes the first viewpoint character. Boss 25 reveals a Legendary Hero Containment Crate, from which the Reader chooses one of the two remaining heroes. Boss 100 frees the final hero from a literal plot hole and completes the party.

Story events do not branch into six separate campaigns. Each page has one canonical event with small participant variants:

- the present heroes receive character-specific lines;
- the missing heroes' lines are omitted rather than reassigned;
- class arrival pages select the correct entrance variant from persisted party order;
- no lore choice changes combat balance or campaign reachability.

## 7. Volume and Page Cadence

One Volume covers 100 enemy levels and contains four 25-level Chapters. The chapter boundaries align with existing Boss levels. Elite victories every five non-Boss levels provide natural short-story beats.

Each Volume contains:

- one opening page when its first frontier level becomes available;
- one page after each first-time frontier victory at a multiple of five;
- four boss sequences at levels ending in 25, 50, 75, and 100;
- one closing page after the Volume boss;
- optional hidden margin notes and Easter eggs that do not affect progression.

Farming an already defeated level never grants duplicate pages. Page availability is derived from frontier and party state wherever possible rather than stored as a second progression ledger.

Story pages are short: normally one animated illustration, two to five sentences, and a few speech bubbles or margin notes. A page is a reward, not a cutscene that blocks idle progression.

## 8. Volume I — The Dungeon of Mandatory Orientation

### 8.1 Levels 1–25: Mandatory Orientation

The Book attempts a respectable fantasy opening. The hero instead meets unionized rats, goblin interns, incorrectly assembled skeletons, giggling mimics, an emotional-support abomination, and Kevin.

Boss 25 is **Gary, Minotaur of Human Resources**. His labyrinth is the HR process; his attacks include Mandatory Fun, Performance Improvement Plan, and a meeting that should have been an email. Defeating him unlocks the second hero and reveals that the alleged Final Page fragment was forwarded to Corporate.

### 8.2 Levels 26–50: Dungeons & Deliverables

The dungeon's warp gate opens into an infinite insurance office that the Book insists qualifies as an evil kingdom. Enemies include Reply-All Swarms, Synergy Slimes, Coffee Elementals, PowerPoint Poltergeists, and middle-management hydras.

Boss 50 is **Chadley von Spreadsheet, Chief Necromancy Officer**. His soul is stored in unread Terms and Conditions. The party defeats him by selecting `Decline All`. He traded the fragment for tickets to an interdimensional anime tournament.

### 8.3 Levels 51–75: The Tournament Arc Nobody Asked For

The party is entered into the Ultimate Supreme Final Tournament of Finality: Preliminary Qualifiers. Ordinary motions gain speed lines, every rival has an excessive flashback, recap monsters summarize recent events, and an accidental beach episode interrupts the bracket.

Boss 75 is **Sebastian Finalform**, whose transformations become visibly cheaper as the animation budget runs out. Red ink deletes part of his final speech and leaves the first direct warning: `THE NARRATOR IS LYING.` Sebastian sold the fragment to a store at the Apocalypse Mall.

### 8.4 Levels 76–100: The Apocalypse Mall

A service elevator opens into a 1990s shopping mall during the end of the universe. The area combines suburban horror, romantic manga, zombie movies, vaporwave, holiday decorations, and anti-shoplifting videos.

Boss 100 is **The Mallgorithm**, a recommendation engine powered by the fragment. It weaponizes observed player behavior, including repeated farming, skipped pages, idle time, and content-setting changes. The party defeats it by asking to speak to its manager; because it is the manager, the request creates infinite recursion.

The final hero escapes from a plot hole. The recovered fragment reads: `DO NOT TRUST THE BOOK. ESPECIALLY WHEN IT USES FOOTNOTES.` The Book declares it counterfeit. The Level 100,000 counter updates to `99,900 LEVELS REMAINING` and a warp gate opens into Volume II.

## 9. Subsequent Story Roadmap

Volume I is the first complete authoring target. Volume II and III receive story briefs here so the initial three-area roadmap has a clear tonal future without requiring hundreds of finished pages before the reader ships.

### 9.1 Volume II, Levels 101–200: A Fistful of Lasers and Leftovers

Genres: space western, competitive cooking anime, and workplace sitcom.

The party boards the underfunded starship **Leftover** and chases an Editor fragment across a frontier where recipes are law. The four chapter bosses are a processed-meat outlaw sheriff, a tyrannical intergalactic cooking judge, a transforming locomotive being robbed while it transforms, and a planet-sized microwave that threatens to reheat the universe unevenly.

The Book repeatedly invents space lore after characters ask basic questions. Kevin runs the ship's cafeteria and remembers food orders from Volume I.

### 9.2 Volume III, Levels 201–300: Magical Girls Don't Get Dental

Genres: magical-girl anime, hard-boiled noir, idol drama, and labor dispute.

The heroes investigate stolen transformation sequences in a rain-soaked city where mascots run protection rackets and every detective monologue attracts sparkles. The four chapter bosses are a corrupt mascot, a vampire idol group, a union-busting kaiju, and the elected Mayor of Sparkletown, who has redacted her own campaign history.

The Editor briefly helps the party restore deleted workers, complicating the Book's villain story.

### 9.3 Future Premise Bank

Later Volumes remain briefs until their implementation milestone:

- **301–400 — The Homeowners Association at the End of the World:** post-apocalypse, suburban HOA, kaiju disaster, and home-renovation television.
- **401–500 — Pirates of the Infinite Help Desk:** pirate opera, cosmic horror, and technical support. The forbidden chant is a ticket number.
- **501–600 — The Extremely Haunted Dating Sim:** romantic visual novel, slasher film, cooking show, and courtroom drama.
- **601–700 — Mecha Pope Championship Wrestling:** giant robots, ecclesiastical bureaucracy, professional wrestling, and sports commentary.
- **701–800 — The Fast and the Feudal:** medieval jousting, street racing, family melodrama, and municipal traffic court.
- **801–900 — Jurassic Tax Season:** dinosaurs, accounting, museum heist, and educational children's television.
- **901–1000 — The Book Goes to Therapy:** every prior genre collapses into a court-ordered group session led by Mercy.

At Level 1000, the party learns that the Book tore out its own Final Page after discovering that its story would someday end. The Editor is the Book's rejected capacity for self-correction, not a simple villain. Kevin remembers revisions because his first printing error placed him in the margin outside normal continuity.

The first-season resolution does not end progression. The party refuses both a fixed ending and endless manipulation. The Book agrees to co-author future Volumes with the cast, immediately relapses, and opens the next warp gate.

## 10. Manga Reader Experience

The manga opens from the management window as a separate story surface. It does not replace the passive rail or interrupt combat.

- Wide windows show a two-page spread.
- Compact windows show one page at a time.
- Reading order and page-turn motion are right to left.
- The index lists Volumes and unlocked Chapters without revealing locked-page adult thumbnails.
- Pages remain rereadable after unlock.
- Book interruptions may overlap panels and margins but cannot cover navigation permanently.
- A reversed `0...10` volume potentiometer lives along the bottom edge of the open Book; its visual labeling does not reveal that `0` is loudest and `10` is quietest.
- VoiceOver controls use explicit `Previous Page` and `Next Page` labels regardless of visual arrow direction.
- VoiceOver exposes the potentiometer's effective volume honestly even though the visual control preserves the joke.
- Keyboard, pointer, and trackpad navigation receive equivalent behavior.
- Closing and reopening returns to the last viewed unlocked page unless that page is no longer valid under a new run, in which case the reader returns to the prologue.

## 11. Limited Animation

Each animated illustration uses at most five authored frames. Loops emphasize manga-page motion rather than full animation: blinking, drifting smoke, trembling text, a rotating arrow, speed lines, a page tear, or Kevin's mustache slipping.

- A loop normally lasts two to six seconds.
- The first frame is a complete static fallback.
- Animation stops when the reader is closed, the application is inactive, or reduced-motion behavior requires a static presentation.
- Animation timing is presentation-only and never reads or changes simulation time.
- Missing optional animation falls back to the static frame rather than making a page unavailable.

## 12. Content Controls

Language, illustration, and spoken-dialogue controls are independent settings.

### 12.1 Language

- **Unfiltered** is the default and uses the authored profane dialogue.
- **Clean** uses separately authored replacement lines and clean Book titles.
- Clean text is a comedic rewrite, not asterisks, silence, or black bars.
- Switching language updates the open page immediately and does not change story progress.

### 12.2 Illustrations

- **Safe** is the default.
- **Adult** requires an explicit local opt-in and confirmation before the first activation.
- Safe and adult versions are intentionally composed alternatives; the safe version is not a blurred adult image.
- Adult variants may use adult nudity and sexual or suggestive comedy, but no sexual violence, coercion, minors, youthful-looking subjects, or essential plot information.
- Platform and storefront content requirements must be reverified before adult assets are authored or shipped.
- Switching illustration mode updates the open page and thumbnails immediately without affecting unlocks.

The Book may react to a changed setting, but repeated toggling cannot create an unbounded dialogue loop.

### 12.3 Spoken Dialogue

- Spoken dialogue is off by default and requires explicit local opt-in.
- Lore speech can play only while the manga Book is visibly open.
- Closing the Book immediately stops current speech and clears its queue.
- Newly unlocked pages may read automatically once after speech is enabled; rereads require Replay.
- A separate versioned dialogue sidecar owns character cues, clean/unfiltered speech, TTS direction, pronunciation, and Book-interaction reactions.
- Moving the Book's reversed volume potentiometer triggers a short Book giggle at the newly selected gain. With speech off, the reaction is visual only.
- Dialogue, voice profiles, reversed-volume behavior, and TTS failure rules are defined in the companion spoken-dialogue design.

## 13. Content Architecture

Lore is bundled, validated content separate from deterministic combat logic.

Conceptual components:

- **LoreCatalog:** all Volumes, pages, variants, animation descriptors, and stable identifiers.
- **LoreCatalogValidator:** duplicate IDs, ordering, milestone coverage, participant references, required text variants, static frames, and asset references.
- **LoreProgressResolver:** derives unlocked pages and class-specific dialogue variants from campaign frontier, party order, and run state.
- **SpokenDialogueCatalog:** speaker profiles, clean/unfiltered cues, delivery direction, pronunciation, and Book-interaction triggers.
- **LoreSpeechService:** provider-neutral TTS playback that is active only while the manga is open.
- **MangaReaderModel:** current index, current page, resolved language/art variants, and reduced-motion state.
- **MangaReaderView:** responsive right-to-left presentation and accessible navigation.

A page definition contains:

- stable page ID and Volume ID;
- ordered page number;
- unlock milestone;
- short unfiltered and clean text variants;
- safe static art and optional adult static art;
- optional four-to-five-frame animation descriptors per art mode;
- optional class-presence dialogue variants;
- accessibility description for each illustration variant.

Required invalid content prevents the affected catalog from becoming active and reports a clear diagnostic. It never mutates the running campaign or save.

## 14. Progress and Persistence

Normal milestone pages derive availability from `CampaignState.highestUnlockedLevel` and `PartyState.heroes`. This avoids duplicating frontier truth in a lore ledger.

The fake-ending prologue and Volume openings use explicit deterministic rules. Hidden Easter eggs are cosmetic and need not persist unless a later achievement design gives them gameplay meaning.

Language mode, illustration mode, spoken-dialogue opt-in, the reversed volume detent, and first-open auto-read belong in versioned `AppSettings`, not `GameState`, because they are application presentation preferences rather than run progression. Extending settings requires a settings-schema migration; it must not modify game-save recovery behavior.

The last viewed page may be stored as disposable reader state. Corruption or absence returns to the newest valid unlocked page or the prologue without affecting the campaign.

## 15. Failure and Edge Behavior

- A missing optional adult variant falls back to the safe variant and labels the page as having no alternate illustration.
- A missing optional animation uses the complete static frame.
- Missing required safe art or both text modes invalidates the affected catalog at startup.
- A page that references a hero not yet present omits that hero's optional dialogue.
- Closing the Book or losing application focus guarantees that no lore speech continues.
- TTS unavailability preserves complete visible dialogue and never blocks a page.
- Rapid potentiometer movement replaces and coalesces giggle previews instead of stacking audio.
- New Game immediately recomputes page availability for the new run; old run pages do not remain unlocked accidentally.
- Farming, automatic retreat, and queued frontier changes never duplicate or revoke valid pages.
- The fake opening never traps assistive-technology users behind intentionally misleading labels; its accessible action remains `Begin Story`, while the visual arrow performs the joke.

## 16. Verification

Focused automated checks must cover:

- catalog validation and deterministic page order;
- one Volume every 100 levels and Chapter boundaries every 25;
- page unlocks from first-time frontier progress and no duplicate farming unlocks;
- starting-class and party-arrival dialogue variants for every class order;
- New Game page recomputation;
- clean/unfiltered text and safe/adult art selection;
- separate spoken-dialogue cue validation and character voice profiles;
- opt-in audio, Book-open-only playback, immediate close interruption, and one-time auto-read;
- reversed potentiometer mapping, persistence, preview replacement, and accessible effective-volume labels;
- settings migration and corrupt-settings fallback without game-save mutation;
- safe fallback when optional adult art or animation is absent;
- reduced-motion static behavior;
- right-to-left pointer, keyboard, and accessible navigation;
- reader closure having no effect on combat or simulation time;
- TTS failure preserving visible story and silence outside the open Book.

Manual verification must inspect the actual rendered reader at wide and compact sizes, the first-run arrow frame-up, Book overlays, page-turn direction, text legibility, animation loops, both censorship controls, and VoiceOver labels. Terminal-only evidence is insufficient for visual acceptance.

## 17. Scope Boundaries

This design does not require:

- story choices that fork combat progression;
- recorded human voice acting or a hard-coded TTS provider;
- full-motion cutscenes;
- online content delivery;
- procedural lore generation;
- persistent tracking for every Easter egg;
- finished pages for all 1,000 outlined levels before the first Volume ships.

The first implementation milestone should prove the prologue, manga reader, settings, spoken-dialogue sidecar, Book-open-only TTS, reversed potentiometer, and a small vertical slice of Volume I before commissioning the complete art and page set.

## 18. Acceptance

The design is successful when:

1. Every 100 levels can host a distinct genre-mashed story without breaking the recurring mystery.
2. Boss 25, Boss 100, party composition, and frontier progress have coherent story consequences.
3. The Book behaves as a funny, unreliable interface character beginning with the reversed-arrow frame-up.
4. Manga pages are short, rereadable, right-to-left, and visually alive with no more than five frames.
5. Profanity and adult illustrations are independently controllable through durable settings.
6. Clean and safe modes remain intentionally funny and complete.
7. Lore cannot alter deterministic combat, damage saves, or block idle progression.
8. Volume I can ship before later premise-bank Volumes are fully authored.
9. Optional TTS gives every character a consistent voice, remains silent outside the open Book, and uses the backwards potentiometer as both volume preview and joke.
